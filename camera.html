RGB camera#

HYPSO-2 carries an RGB camera next to the hyperspectral imager. Each capture directory holds its image as <capture id>-rgb/<target>_<time>.bmp: one 8-bit raw RGGB Bayer mosaic, 3840 x 2748 pixels (about 30 m), taken about 2 s before the HSI capture. It has no georeferencing of its own.

Reading#

from hypso.rgb import find_camera_image, read_camera_image

camera = read_camera_image(find_camera_image(capture_dir))   # (y, x, channel) R, G, B

The mosaic is demosaiced (bilinear, RGGB) and rotated 180 degrees, as the ground processing’s rgb-camera-image-debayered.png. Reading never registers the image.

Registration#

Registration is an explicit step that takes the geolocation from the HSI capture:

from hypso.rgb import register_camera, georeference_camera

l1d = hypso.open(l1d_path, wavelengths=[640, 550, 460])   # three bands suffice
reg = register_camera(camera, l1d)
reg.ok, reg.inliers, reg.rms_m, reg.coverage
geo = georeference_camera(camera, reg, l1d)      # R, G, B, latitude, longitude, in_hsi_swath

How it works: the HSI bands near red, green and blue are regridded onto a UTM map (150 m); SIFT keypoints are matched between that map and the camera image; RANSAC fits a homography from camera pixels to map coordinates (a frame camera looking at nearly flat ground maps onto the ground plane by a homography). The fit must have at least 30 inliers, cover at least a quarter of the camera image (a fit anchored in one corner is unreliable elsewhere) and have a residual RMS of at most 500 m; otherwise reg.ok is false with the reason and the image is not georeferenced. On the test captures the residual is about 190-220 m, well below the HSI’s 876 x 108 m pixels, in about 40 s.

hsi_to_camera_pixels and camera_to_hsi_pixels map between the two images in their own pixel coordinates. Camera pixels outside the HSI swath are georeferenced by extrapolating the fit (in_hsi_swath is 0 there).

Maps#

from hypso.rgb import regrid_camera, plot_rgb_map

on_hsi_grid = regrid_camera(geo, l1d.regrid(resolution=300).area_definition())
own_grid = regrid_camera(geo)                     # UTM, the camera's ~30 m
plot_rgb_map(own_grid, graticule=True, scale_bar=True, north_arrow=True, coastlines=True)

regrid_camera averages the camera pixels in each cell when cells are at least twice the camera’s pixel size and takes the nearest pixel otherwise. plot_rgb_map draws any gridded RGB image (a regridded camera image or a regridded hypso.Product) with optional latitude/longitude graticule, scale bar, north arrow, coastlines (needs cartopy and its Natural Earth data) and title. Both need matplotlib, which the package does not depend on.

Exposure#

The camera’s exposure varies a lot between captures: a winter capture can be 2-3 stops too dark, a bright summer one saturated in blue. adjust_exposure() changes the brightness of any RGB image (camera image, camera product, HSI2RGB output) with one gain for all channels, so colours are kept:

from hypso.rgb import adjust_exposure

brighter = adjust_exposure(camera, ev=1.5)                       # +1.5 stops
auto = adjust_exposure(camera)                                   # 99th percentile -> 90% of full scale
natural = adjust_exposure(camera, black_point="channel", normalize=True)   # 0-1, for plotting
plot_rgb_map(own_grid, exposure={"black_point": "channel"})

ev is in stops (gain 2**ev); without it the gain is automatic. black_point subtracts a dark level first: "channel" each channel’s 1st percentile (removes the camera’s dark offset and atmospheric haze, usually the most natural look over water), "common" one level for all channels (keeps the colour balance), or values. gamma applies a display gamma. A saturated channel cannot be restored: on a capture whose blue is clipped, per-channel black levels turn the image yellow, so use "common" or none there. The result is for display; it is float32 and records exposure_gain, exposure_ev, exposure_gamma and exposure_black. plot_rgb_map(..., exposure={...}) uses it instead of its per-channel percentile stretch.

Camera products#

The camera is not a hypso.Product (no wavelengths, calibration levels or SRFs); a camera product is an xarray Dataset in the same CF/ACDD conventions:

from hypso.rgb import camera_product, write_camera, open_camera

camera_l1a = camera_product(camera, reg, l1d)    # reg may be None or failed
path = write_camera(camera_l1a, out_dir=".")     # HYPSO2_RGB.20250404T022723.ariake.L1A.nc
camera_l1a = open_camera(path)

R, G and B are the demosaiced counts in the camera’s own frame, stored as uint8 (the bilinear demosaic rounded). L1A means uncalibrated counts. A registered product adds float32 latitude/longitude per pixel, in_hsi_swath (when the HSI product is given) and the registration attributes; an unregistered one records why, without geolocation (georeferenced = "false"). The file name uses the RGB instrument token (Products and files); a regrid_camera() result gets the grid token too (...L1A.utm52n_100m.nc). About 22 MB for a registered 3840 x 2748 image.

Satpy#

The hypso_rgb_l1a reader matches _RGB names only (the HSI readers match _HSI) and has a true_color composite from R, G and B:

from satpy import Scene

scene = Scene(reader="hypso_rgb_l1a", filenames=[str(path)])
scene.load(["true_color"])

A native-frame product is a swath (per-pixel latitude/longitude), a regridded one is read on its map grid. Unregistered products cannot be placed by Satpy.
