Footprints and maps#

hypso.maps makes a capture’s ground footprint (as a polygon, a shapefile or GeoJSON), RGB images and maps of any product variable. It needs shapely for footprints, pyshp for shapefiles, matplotlib for figures and cartopy (with its Natural Earth data) for coastlines; none is a dependency of the package.

Footprints#

import hypso
from hypso import maps

l1d = hypso.open("HYPSO2_HSI.20261006T022612.koreanCalValUB03.L1D.nc", wavelengths=[460, 550, 640])
l1d.footprint()                          # shapely Polygon, WGS84 lon/lat: the swath outline
l1d.footprint(mask="data")               # only the pixels with data (blocks of 8 x 8)
l1d.write_footprint("footprint.shp")     # .shp .shx .dbf .prj .cpg, EPSG:4326
maps.write_footprints([a, b, c], "all.geojson")   # one feature per capture

The swath outline traces the outer ring of the pixel grid (a vertex every 10 pixels). Across the antimeridian, longitudes are given in 0-360. Each feature carries the capture’s metadata (field names of at most 10 characters for the shapefile format):

capture

<target>_<YYYY-MM-DDTHH-MM-SSZ>, as on the HYPSO servers

satellite

HYPSO-1 or HYPSO-2

target

the capture’s target name

start_utc

acquisition start

level

L1C, L1D, L2A, L2B

product

e.g. L1D, L2A.RRS.ocsmart, L2B.BGC.ocsmart

georef

indirect (GCP-corrected), direct or unknown

calib

radiometric calibration file

ac_method

atmospheric correction (L2)

lines

swath size

samples

area_km2

footprint area (geodesic)

centre_lat

mean pixel position

centre_lon

source

file name, when read from a file

RGB images#

maps.save_rgb(product, "rgb.png") writes an RGB PNG (bands nearest 640, 550 and 460 nm) in sensor geometry, transparent where there is no data, in the HYPSO servers’ quicklook orientation (orientation="product" for the arrays as stored; bin_samples=3 like the servers’ -bin3 images). L1 products are stretched per channel (2-98 percentiles, one gain for all channels renders TOA land pink) with a display gamma of 2.2. L2A surface reflectance (Rrs, rhos) gets one linear gain for all channels, to the 99.5th percentile: true colour, deep blue for clear ocean and green for turbid lakes (a gamma or a per-channel stretch washes it out to flat cyan).

Maps#

maps.plot_map(l1d)                                   # RGB
maps.plot_map(hypso.open(rrs_file, wavelengths=[560]), "Rrs", 560)
maps.plot_map(hypso.open(bgc_file), "chlor_a_oci")   # log scale for concentrations and IOPs
maps.plot_overview([p1, p2, p3])                     # footprints of several captures

plot_map reduces the product to what it plots, regrids it (Product.regrid, nearest neighbour, UTM, the product’s resolution) and draws it over light-grey land and light-blue sea and lakes (Natural Earth 10 m ocean and lakes; water=False to switch off), with the footprint outline, coastlines and lake shores, a latitude/longitude graticule, scale bar, north arrow and colour bar. plot_overview draws footprint outlines in distinct colours with the legend beside the map.

For processed capture folders, demo/make_maps.py writes all of these at once:

demo/make_maps.py ~/HYPSO_DATA/koreanCalVal* --overview ~/HYPSO_DATA/overview --group korea=koreanCalVal
